ADULT EDUCATION & DEVELOPMENTAL EDUCATION 
PROGRAM DESCRIPTION
The Adult Basic Education (ABE) and Developmental Education Program serves as SIPI's Adult Education 
department and provides the academic foundation for student success at Southwestern Indian Polytechnic 
Institute. As a gateway to higher education, the department prepares adult learners to begin or continue their 
educational journey by strengthening the knowledge, skills, and confidence needed for college, careers, and lifelong 
learning.
The program provides individualized instruction that strengthens foundational skills in reading, writing, 
mathematics, digital literacy, and academic success while preparing students for college-level coursework, 
workforce training, high school equivalency, certificate programs, and associate degree pathways. Instruction 
emphasizes critical thinking, effective communication, problem-solving, workforce readiness, and the learning 
strategies necessary for success in both academic and professional settings.
Consistent with Southwestern Indian Polytechnic Institute's mission, the department expands educational access 
through culturally responsive, student-centered instruction that honors the strengths, experiences, and aspirations 
of Native American students. Whether students are earning a high school equivalency credential, preparing for 
college coursework, developing workforce skills, or advancing toward new educational and career opportunities, 
the program provides the foundation for continued growth and success.
Mission Statement
The mission of the Adult Basic Education and Developmental Education Program is to provide accessible, high￾quality Adult Education that strengthens foundational academic skills, prepares students for postsecondary 
education, workforce training, and employment, and promotes lifelong learning through culturally responsive 
instruction that serves Native American students and tribal communities.
Goals 
Program participants will:
• Earn a high school equivalency credential and/or strengthen foundational academic skills.
• Successfully transition into college-level coursework, workforce training, certificate or degree 
programs, or employment.
• Develop the academic, digital, critical thinking, and professional skills necessary for lifelong learning.
• Strengthen their ability to contribute to their families, tribal communities, and the broader workforce 
through continued education and career advancement.
Learning Outcomes
Students completing the program will be able to:
• Demonstrate skill gains in reading, writing, mathematics, and digital literacy appropriate to their 
educational goals.
• Apply critical thinking and problem-solving skills in academic, workplace, and everyday situations.
• Communicate effectively in academic, professional, and community settings.
• Demonstrate the study skills, self-management, and learning strategies necessary for success in college￾level coursework and workforce training.
• Develop and implement an educational and career plan that supports continued learning and employment.

ADULT BASIC EDUCATION & DEVELOPMENTAL EDUCATION 
Program Description 
SIPI’s Adult Basic Education (ABE) and Developmental Education program seeks to ensure that students have 
every opportunity to be successful in achieving their individual goals. The ABE goal is to provide students with the 
basic educational and life skills required for completing their High School Equivalence Certificate (passing either 
the HiSET or GED tests), ABE and/or Developmental Education Programs, which will prepare the student to enter 
or retain employment and/or continue to a degree or certificate program. 
The ability for students to better meet these goals is aligned with SIPI’s mission and its commitment to develop 
long-term goals to ensure support and assistance to Tribal communities. SIPI’s affirmation of the belief that each 
person’s worth and dignity is of the utmost importance while attending the institution is reflected throughout the 
program. 
SIPI’s ABE and Developmental Education programs have competencies and learning objectives aligned with the 
institutional mission as well as the State of New Mexico’s Adult Basic Education program. 
Program Goal 
“SIPI students will have the foundational building blocks that will allow them to transfer and succeed in college￾level courses.”

ACCOUNTING PROGRAM 
ACCOUNTING — ASSOCIATE OF APPLIED SCIENCE DEGREE 
The AAS Degree in Accounting is a program of study over five semesters designed to prepare graduates with the 
general skills necessary to obtain an entry-level position in the field of accounting or work toward completion of 
a Bachelor’s Degree in Accounting. A formal double-entry accounting system is taught using manual and 
computerized systems. Generally accepted accounting principles and concepts are emphasized. The Tribal 
Accounting class will further prepare students to successfully obtain employment with the tribes. 
According to the latest U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) employment projections for the 2024–2034 
decade, Accountants and Auditors will add 72,800 new jobs, while Management Analysts will add 94,500 new jobs, 
for a combined total of 167,300 additional jobs across these occupations. 
Mission Statement 
The Accounting Associate of Applied Science Program will provide training and education to students to enable 
them to compete and qualify for employment in the accounting field; and to produce well-trained and competent 
professionals to provide services to private, public, and tribal entities. 
Goals and Program Outcomes 
Program graduates will obtain an entry-level position in the accounting field 
Program graduates will continue their higher education studies 
Associate of Applied Science Degree in Accounting Outcomes: 
Program graduates will be able to 
• Prepare financial statements and record business transactions per Generally Accepted Accounting 
Principles 
• Analyze financial statements using ratio analysis and managerial accounting techniques 
• Apply various business and accounting software applications to accounting processes 
• Demonstrate an understanding of income tax preparation processes for an individual tax return. 
• Apply ethical principles in business decision-making.

BUSINESS EDUCATION 
Mission Statement
Within a culturally sensitive learning environment, the Business Education mission is to educate and prepare 
professional Native American business students to become informed decision-makers, manage complex business 
operations, and contribute to commerce and business in the global market.
Goals
Improving the number of graduates enrolling in the certificate or degree programs in Accounting and Business 
Administration to enter the business world.
Empower students to pursue higher education and assist with the transition into a four-year college or university. 
Capitalize on the services of a professional business advisory committee comprised of individuals from business 
and government agencies and leverage their expertise and resources to enhance student opportunities.
Build strong tribal, state, and federal partnerships to strengthen tribal communities in areas such as economic 
development, leadership, and entrepreneurship.
Expand articulation agreements with four-year institutions of higher education for course-transfer opportunities 
for Business Education students.
Students may select from certificate programs or associate degree offerings. Students who complete a certificate 
program may apply those credits toward a related associate degree program, subject to program requirements. 
Based on certain criteria, a student may enter directly into a degree program.
Most courses in the Associate of Science degree programs are transferable to other two-year and four-year 
institutions of higher education in New Mexico and appear on the New Mexico Statewide Articulation Matrix. 
Students should work closely with advisors and transfer institutions to ensure successful transfer.
Note: Some courses may have prerequisites. Refer to the catalog and/or consult an advisor.

The Associate of Science degree in Business Administration is a program of study designed to meet the educational

CONSERVATION AND RESOURCE PROTECTION
The Conservation and Resource Protection Certificate of Achievement prepares students to support the protection, 
monitoring, and stewardship of natural and cultural resources, particularly within Tribal communities. This 
program is designed for individuals seeking to enter or advance in careers related to conservation monitoring, 
resource compliance support, land protection, or Tribal environmental programs. 
Through coursework in forest and natural resources, environmental law and policy, professional writing, and 
cultural resource protection, students develop the skills needed to work respectfully and effectively at the 
intersection of natural resources management and Tribal governance. The certificate is especially relevant for 
those serving as or preparing to become Tribal conservation officers, resource monitors, or environmental 
compliance staff.
Students learn through classroom instruction, case studies, applied projects, and community-based examples 
relevant to conservation and resource protection. The program is designed to provide a strong foundation for 
entry-level positions while also supporting continued education in natural resources, environmental science, 
conservation, and related fields.
Mission Statement
The mission of the Conservation and Resource Protection Certificate is to strengthen the capacity of Tribal 
communities to protect and manage their natural and cultural resources through education that emphasizes 
sovereignty, respect, and professional practice. The program prepares students to work effectively in foundational 
roles related to conservation, environmental education, and land stewardship by integrating legal knowledge, 
cultural values, and communication skills.
Goals
Graduates of the certificate program will:
1. Understand the legal and policy foundations of natural and cultural resource protection, including federal 
environmental laws and Tribal governance frameworks.
2. Develop technical writing and reporting skills necessary for documenting compliance, enforcement actions, and 
environmental assessments.
3. Demonstrate awareness of the cultural and historical significance of places, landscapes, and natural features, and 
apply respectful strategies for their protection.
4. Prepare for roles in Tribal natural resource departments, compliance programs, or land protection efforts, with a 
strong foundation in both Western regulatory systems and Indigenous values.
Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this certificate program, students will be able to:
1) Describe key U.S. environmental and conservation laws and how they apply to Tribal land and resource 
management.
2) Develop professional-quality reports, including investigative summaries, compliance documentation, and 
management plans tailored to various audiences.
3) Describe the ecological, cultural, and economic importance of forest and natural resources to Tribal 
communities.
4) Explain the role of Tribal sovereignty and governance in shaping conservation and environmental policies.
5) Implement approaches to protecting cultural and historic sites that integrate traditional knowledge and 
practice into resource stewardship.

GEOSPATIAL INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY
The purpose of the Geospatial Information Technology (GIT) Program is to provide technical skills to use geo￾referenced data for the purpose of economic, educational, and social development of Native American 
communities. The technical skills are achieved through short, intensive courses as well as regular, semester-long 
courses, and community-based demonstration projects at SIPI.
Geospatial Information Technology (GIT) takes the locations of our lives and maps them, utilizing methods and 
technology from Geography, Mathematics, and Computer Science. SIPI’s GIT students learn skills to help them work 
in a variety of fields. Our students work on exercises ranging from resource management to economic development 
to homeland security. SIPI’s GIT program prepares students to enter the workforce and to be leaders in their 
communities. The GIT program also offers research internships, where students research a wide range of topics.
SIPI’s Science and Technology building houses state-of-the-art computer facilities, laboratories, and classrooms 
that provide an excellent teaching and learning environment for the GIT Program. Students will learn to use the 
latest software and equipment when learning both geographical information systems (GIS) and geographic 
positioning systems (GPS).
Students who enjoy science and computers can make a significant difference and have a well-paid job by choosing 
a career in geospatial information technology. Graduates could be a part of efforts to conserve, manage, and 
improve resources such as wildlife, fisheries, forests, rangelands, and water, or go to work for businesses analyzing 
their target markets, product distribution network, or financial lending patterns. SIPI students in GIT obtain a 
sound academic foundation and get extensive real-world experience.
GIT graduates are well prepared to transfer their Associate of Applied Science degrees to four-year universities 
and complete their Bachelor of Science degrees. SIPI has transfer programs in place that help students complete 
the transition to their four-year school. Career opportunities for students in geospatial fields are excellent. Most 
graduates are employed by federal land management agencies, local tribes, or local industry.
The geospatial industry has been identified by the Department of Labor as a high-growth industry. To provide 
training and learning opportunities for both students new to the field as well as professionals, SIPI offers two 
different programs in geospatial technology: an Associate of Applied Science degree and a Certificate in GIT.
Mission Statement
The purpose of the Geospatial Information Technology Program is to teach skills that enable students to 
collect, maintain, store, analyze, and present geo-referenced data. In addition to learning about the technical 
applications of geographic information systems (GIS) and geographic positioning systems (GPS), students will 
also have the opportunity to practice and build upon general education competency skills in both the classroom 
and internship positions. The combination of a strong foundation of GIT technical skills as well as the opportunity 
to further develop general education competency skills ensures that graduates become lifelong learners who can 
successfully pursue either a four-year degree or a job in the GIT field.

NATURAL RESOURCES MANAGEMENT
The Natural Resources Management Program prepares students to work in a variety of technical-level fields in 
natural resources. To ensure student success in academic and workplace settings, students must have a solid 
general education foundation. In addition to the required General Education requirements, the Natural Resources 
curriculum provides individuals with technical training in plant and tree identification, geospatial applications, 
natural resource measurements and techniques, and soils.
Students learn through a variety of approaches, including classroom instruction, internships, and laboratory and 
field experiences. Natural Resources (NATR) students have opportunities to gain hands-on experience in a variety 
of learning environments that incorporate the campus greenhouses, classroom and laboratory facilities, and 
arboretum, orchard, and agriculture fields. In addition to the Rio Grande Bosque, the surrounding mountains, 
rangeland, and riparian areas provide natural resources students with practical outdoor field training 
opportunities.
Mission Statement
The Natural Resources Management Program will provide students with current knowledge and skills to be

knowledge to perform successfully in tribal, private, non-government organizations (NGOs), state, and federal 
organizations, as well as transfer successfully into bachelor’s degree programs. Graduates will leave with the 
knowledge and skills required to meet current employment standards.
Goals
The primary goal of the Natural Resources Program curriculum is to prepare students to enter the field at a 
technical level with the knowledge and skills required to meet current employment standards while recognizing 
and supporting academic achievement options through articulation.

designed to be responsive to tribal, public, and federal agency needs. The first year of the program involves general 
education requirements and program core courses, and the second year is devoted to intensive specialized 
coursework and field labs to meet specific training needs in resource management. The curriculum is designed to 
provide a technical background for graduates to assist professionals in the field or laboratory and to provide a 
strong academic program for students planning to pursue higher degrees. Many program courses are accepted 
directly or as electives upon transfer to universities within New Mexico and Arizona.
The Associate in Applied Science (AAS) degree program in Natural Resources Management is designed to provide 
students with the skills needed to enter the workforce directly. The curriculum includes a required general 
education component, a science foundation, field techniques, and supervised internship experience. To receive the 
AAS degree, a minimum grade of C is required in all coursework.
Natural Resources Management Degree Goals
• Students will be able to work as technicians in the field of Natural Resources.

Natural Resources Management Degree Outcomes

• Demonstrate technical knowledge of natural resources through oral and written communication.

NETWORK MANAGEMENT The Network Management curriculum is based on the CISCO Systems Networking Academy Program and has two 
primary focuses: Networking and System Administration. Students are guided to understand end-user

VISION CARE TECHNOLOGY 
Program Description 
The Vision Care Technology Program is a comprehensive formal education and training program in the field of 
vision care. Program courses are designed to lead students into careers as opticians, ophthalmic dispensers, 
ophthalmic optical laboratory technicians, or ophthalmic technicians/assistants. 
The foundation of the Vision Care Technology Program is based on the specialized skill trade of opticianry. 
Students receive theoretical and laboratory experiences in optics and engage in supervised practical experiences. 
Students engage and identify specific eye care needs through the analysis of an eye doctor's refractive correction 
prescription. 
The Associate Degree prepares students for national certification examinations administered by the American 
Board of Opticianry (ABO) and National Contact Lens Examiners (NCLE). 
The program also offers a Certificate of Completion in Optical Laboratory Technology. 
Goals 
• The primary goal of the Vision Care Technology Programs is to educate all individuals on technical 
competencies and capabilities to enable them to compete and qualify for employment in the optical and 
ophthalmic industry. 
• The program also strives to eliminate hazardous waste and reduce non-hazardous waste to minimum 
levels where economically and technically practical, and to be in full compliance with all federal and state 
environmental regulations. 
VISION CARE TECHNOLOGY — ASSOCIATE OF APPLIED SCIENCE DEGREE 
Mission Statement 
The Vision Care Technology Program will provide training and education to students to enable them to compete 
and qualify for employment in the Ophthalmic/Opticianry/Optical field; and to produce well-trained, competent 
professionals to provide professional services to the public. 
Program Outcomes 
• Interpret prescriptions written by licensed practitioners. 
• Use communication effectively using listening, speaking, reading and writing skills. 
• Describe managerial operations, policies/procedures, and teamwork concepts. 
• Demonstrate knowledge of clinical procedures in an optometric/ophthalmic practice.
